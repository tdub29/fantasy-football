The one assumption that decides it

Everything long-run rests on the relation that turns a pick into the points its rookie scores. It prices your 2028 first at about 143 points in year one and a fourth at about 45. Scale that up for every roster, so nobody gets an unfair edge, and the seven-year answer inverts.

Three things keep that from being a reason to refuse. The 2026 column does not move at all across the whole sweep, because a pick that has not converted cannot play this season, so the win-now half is arithmetic rather than assumption. 1.3x is probably too generous, since it makes every fourth-rounder a 60-point rookie against a documented hit rate near 8 percent for third rounders and later. And you are pick-rich, so any upward revision in what picks are worth helps you more than the field, which is the same fact as your picks being expensive because your team is bad.

What it does mean: do not send this one standalone. A trade that leaves you 8th, costs an early first, and needs a calibration to hold within seven percent does not stand by itself. Paired with Deal A it is a contender’s move and the arithmetic stops being close.

Where this could be wrong

  • The simulator has no waiver pool and no trade market, so depth is worth more in the model than in the league. That is exactly why the QB counters read worse than instinct says.
  • Injury rates are positional, not personal. The Watson table above is a sensitivity, not a rerun.
  • Every roster in the pull is 0-0. This is all projection with none of the season in it.
  • Trust 2026 through 2028. The out-years oscillate by more than the differences between them.
  • Your 2028 1st is worth 5,545 because you project third worst. Win five games and it is closer to 4,800.
