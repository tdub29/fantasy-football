## 2. Comparability: partly stated, two gaps

- **Paired tables are fair.** Each paired class uses the intersection of drafted players matched by both boards.
- **The per-source table is not a ranking.** Its rows cover different classes, positions and board depths:
  - CBS 2013 is RB only (ALL coverage 0.237), and CBS 2016 is QB plus TE only.
  - ESPN 2018 is QB plus TE only, which are the high rho positions.
  - The NFLMDD 2020 board is 150 deep (coverage 0.59; TE rho is -0.107 on n=7).
  - results.md does not say this next to the table.
- **"7 of 7" carries no interval.** results.md says "no interval is claimed", which is adequate. CHECKPOINT line 158
  repeats "NFLMDD higher in 7 of 7" with no caveat. The per-class gaps are +0.062, 0.120, 0.170, 0.141, 0.078, 0.106
  and 0.033: consistent in sign, but each one is a single class.
- **The granularity explanation is stated as fact and is mostly not supported.** results.md lines 33-34 say "Part of
  that gap is granularity".
  - The test: within position, on the same drafted players for 2020-2026, WF's own position rank has no ties.
  - Mean rho is 0.656 for WF position rank, 0.640 for the WF round signal and 0.749 for NFLMDD. NFLMDD is higher in 21
    of 28 cells.
  - So ties explain about 0.016 of a gap near 0.10.
- **Two confounds are unmentioned.**
  - Vintage: NFLMDD captures are 1 to 22 days before the draft. The freshest WF ALL capture is 19, 23, 47, 37, 26, 16
    and 4 days out for 2020-2026, and some WF position stamps are up to 145 days old.
  - Source type: NFLMDD is a consensus of many boards, while WF is one analyst.
  - Either confound alone could produce the gap.
- **"CBS ties it" (results.md line 35) overstates agreement.** The pooled means match, but the per-class differences
  run from -0.098 to +0.132 on five classes. "No detectable difference" is the defensible wording.

## 3. Coverage gaps and selection: one bias not stated

- **Drafted-only conditioning is stated** (results.md lines 13-14).
- **Depth inflates rho, and this is not stated.** Truncating WF to pos_rank <= 25 raises the pooled rho in 12 of 15
  classes, with the mean going from 0.720 to 0.739. So shallow boards (CBS top 25, truncated ESPN pages) gain about
  +0.02 from depth alone against deep boards.
  - ESPN QB and TE pages are full lists (pageCount 1).
  - ESPN 2019 RB (pageCount 2) and WR (pageCount 3) captures are page 1 only.
- **Coverage above 1 is possible and unexplained.** Three cells show it: WF 2012 TE 1.083, NFLMDD 2021 TE 1.091 and
  NFLMDD 2026 RB 1.083. Board position is taken from the page label, while the denominator uses draft position. For
  example, Rhett Ellison is TE on the board but RB in the draft data, and Jacob Harris is TE on the board but WR in the
  draft data.
- **Six WF players sit on two position pages and are counted in both position rows.** They are 2015 Devin Gardner
  QB/WR, 2016 Bralon Addison, Byron Marshall and one RB/WR whose surname this repo's slop hook rejects (find him with a
  duplicate (year, name) query on wf_predraft.csv), 2017 Curtis Samuel RB/WR, and 2021 Demetric Felton RB/WR.
  ALL dedupes them by pick.
- **New finding: the fallback name match creates false matches.** The fallback (capital_wayback.py lines 330 and
  347) matches on first initial plus last name plus position whenever the exact name misses. The false matches it
  produced:
  - WF 2020: J.J. Taylor matched to Jonathan Taylor (pick 41), and Hunter Bryant to Harrison Bryant (pick 115).
  - WF 2018: Darrel Williams matched to David Williams.
  - NFLMDD: Hunter Bryant (2020) to Harrison Bryant; Jaylen Harris (2021) to Jacob Harris; ZaQuandre White (2022) to
    Zamir White; Ty Scott and Jyaire Shorter (2023) to Tyler Scott and Justin Shorter; Je'Quan Burton and Marcus
    Washington (2024) to Jermaine Burton and Malik Washington; Ayden Williams (2026) to Antonio Williams.
  - How the dedupe resolves them: line 355 keeps the first row in file order for each pick. Where the impostor ranks
    above the real player, the impostor's signal is used. Examples are WF Hunter Bryant (rank 6, 3-5) against Harrison
    Bryant (rank 12, 4-6), and NFLMDD Hunter Bryant (76) against Harrison Bryant (88).
  - Rows that use a false match: about 10 of roughly 1,000 matched pairs, so the pooled means should move little.
    Single class cells can move. My dict-order reproduction gave 0.739 for WF 2020 ALL against the eval's 0.764.
  - The genuine nickname fallbacks (Pat Mahomes, Cam Ward, Chig Okonkwo, KC Concepcion) are correct and worth keeping.

## 4. ESPN 2018 title-year fix: correct, fails safe, selftest partial

- **The fix reads real pages correctly.** Real 2018 titles read "NFL Draft 2018 Best Remaining Players - ESPN
  Draftcast". Each 2019 page carries exactly one `"draftYear"` key, and it agrees with the title.
- **It fails safe.** A variant title returns None, and load() then skips the page. When key and title conflict, the
  key wins.
- **The URL carries no year.** The class therefore comes from the CDX window (Y-1 0901 to draft day) plus the `dy != y`
  check.
- **The selftest passes and does exercise the title fallback (line 417).** It does not cover a key/title conflict or
  the skip path in load(). The draftYear regex scans the whole page, not only the bestAvailable block. That is harmless
  on the captured pages.

## 5. Leakage: claim reproduced, spec clean, one population caveat

- **The DOB presence leak reproduces on the c5d39d02 copy (immutable).** DOB presence, drafted vs undrafted:
  - 2015: 49.3 vs 3.0
  - 2018: 96.0 vs 4.4
  - 2022: 100 vs 6.2
  - 2024: 100 vs 4.6
  - Height and weight sit at 98 to 100 in both groups.
- **Caveat on the lane's own run.** The lane's leak check ran on the older 5b948640 copy, which comes from the same
  player_link run. "Drafted" there means linked players only.
- **The spec is clean.** forecaster-spec line 66 excludes `cfb_roster.date_of_birth` and `age`. The age proxy at line
  59 is D minus recruit class season, and it is fixed before college, so it carries no presence leak.
- **The population definition leaks timing.** forecaster-spec lines 37-39 define class D candidates as players whose
  last roster season is D-1 and call this "not look-ahead". But absence from the season D roster is only observable
  after the draft. Backtests are fine; real-time use needs declaration or early-entry lists.
