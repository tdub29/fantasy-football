## Findings

1. **R:247, D DOB by games: MISMATCH.** Claim 78.9 / 77.9 / 77.3 / 85.4%. Recompute 74.3 (165/222) / 77.9 /
   77.3 / 87.7 (434/495). The middle two match; the ends do not, under any variant tried (any DOB, all rows,
   feature rows). "D is flatter" also fails on comparable classes: the D 0-games bucket holds 102 class
   2024-26 rows with no NFL seasons yet. Restricted to classes through 2023, D runs 55.0 (66/120) / 72.2 /
   74.5 / 87.7%, as steep as U. Correction: replace the D line with the recomputed figures and drop "D is
   flatter"; DOB presence tracks NFL tenure in both populations.

2. **R:194, unlinked U by conference: MISMATCH.** Claim "Only 4 of the 177 are P5 and 1 is G5". Recompute P5 3,
   G5 2, which is also what the table at R:167-168 says (2/189 G5, 3/370 P5). Correct the sentence.

3. **R:99 (and the fix at R:282-283), rule-conflict pattern: MISMATCH with R:86-95.** The table is right
   (every id, rule, season, team and DOB presence reproduced from `cfb_roster`). The summary "seasons in
   sequence at different schools, and a DOB on only one of the two ids" holds for 5 of 10. Crowell and Nic
   Jacobs have a DOB on neither id. Natson's two ids share both schools and both hold 2016; Tonga's share team
   252 and both hold 2009; Myles White's share team 2348. So 7 of 10 look like transfer re-keys (sequential,
   no shared team), and 3 look like duplicate ids, not transfers. The fix-1 merge rule as written ("distinct
   listed schools", "in sequence") would reach the 7, not Natson, Tonga or White; R:283 lists Natson as a
   rescue. "Not certified" is stated honestly at R:100, R:283 and R:327. Correction: say 7 of 10 fit a
   transfer re-key, 3 look like same-school duplicate ids, and scope fix 1 to match.

4. **R:205, C:72-73, "missing by school level and era, not by NFL result": OVERSTATED.** The marginal games
   rates do reproduce (10.7 / 10.2 / 7.0 / 10.3%). Within class 2008-11, the unlinked rate falls with games:
   19.4% (41/211) at 0 games, 12.0, 8.2, then 7.4% (2/27) at 49+. 2012-15 and 2016-19 are flat; 2020-23 cells
   are small. The flat marginal partly reflects older classes having both more games and more unlinked rows.
   Correction: "no marginal outcome skew; in 2008-11 unlinked rows skew to players with no NFL games."

5. **R:190 and R:324, conference as the school-level proxy: missing caveat.** The limit says 1008 U rows lack a
   conference. It does not say the gap itself tracks outcome and era: missing for 68.7% (625/910) at 0 games
   down to 30.2% (38/126) at 49+, and for 94.3% of 2008-11 down to 1.8% of 2020-23. The D2-lower vs P5 contrast
   rests on a 43% subset that skews recent and toward NFL tenure. The direction of the finding is still
   plausible (the named unlinked producers are D2/D3 schools), but it should carry this caveat.

6. **R:242-252, DOB and "join keys only": consequence correct, add two things.** The 51.8 to 74.3% gradient is
   not only era mix: within class it rises in 2012-15 (26.6 to 59.0%) and 2016-19 (15.9 to 80.5%), is flat in
   2008-11 (86.5 to 80.0%) and high in 2020-23. So DOB presence and a dob_name method encode outcome, and
   keeping them out of the feature set is right. Missing: (a) the label at R:248 "DOB presence" quotes exact-DOB
   rates (32.2, 92.3); any-DOB is 34.1 and 94.4, and 2008-11 is 83.8 exact, so the era swing is non-monotone;
   (b) "join key only" governs features, not link precision. Rule precision was checked only on the 1148
   ESPN-id rows (R:76); new U links at 0 games have the least DOB corroboration, so a per-games-bucket check of
   wrong-person links would close the loop. Recommendation, not a mismatch.

7. **R:175-176, U recruit match: near MATCH, definitional.** Claim none 160/638 (25.1%), one no-feature 9.1%.
   Recompute none 160/639 (25.0%), one 17/1055 (1.6%, MATCH), one no-feature 9.0%. One row moves between "none"

8. **R:200, recruit stars: MATCH on means, n mislabeled.** 2.86 and 3.14 reproduce, but they average the 14 and

9. **R:146, D "only one is P5": MATCH on the conference field.** Rico Gathers (Baylor, a basketball convert) is a
