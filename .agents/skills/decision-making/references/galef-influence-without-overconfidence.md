# Galef: Influence Without Overconfidence

## When to Use

Use when the user needs to communicate uncertainty without sounding lost:

- Portfolio or product memo with ranges and caveats.
- SMB seller/lender/investor conversation.
- Stakeholder update after a noisy result.
- A plan where certainty would be dishonest but hesitation would lose trust.

## The Framework

Galef distinguishes epistemic confidence from social confidence. You can be socially steady while epistemically honest. The trick is to justify uncertainty, give informed estimates, and pair uncertainty with a plan.

## How to Apply

1. **Separate confidence types.** What do we know vs how should we present?
2. **Name the uncertainty.** Is uncertainty in the world or in our information?
3. **Give informed estimates.** Use ranges, scenarios, or confidence levels.
4. **Justify the uncertainty.** Explain why the unknowns are real.
5. **Show the plan.** What are we doing despite uncertainty?
6. **Draft the message.** Calm, specific, non-defensive.

## Output Format

```markdown
## Influence Without Overconfidence: {message}

What we know:
What is uncertain:
Why uncertainty is justified:
Informed estimate:
Plan:
Draft wording:
```

## Common Failure Modes

- **Fake certainty.** Overstating confidence to look competent.
- **Apologetic uncertainty.** Treating honesty as weakness.
- **Vague hedging.** "Maybe" without ranges, reasons, or plan.
- **No plan.** Uncertainty without action undermines trust.
