# Kahneman — Cognitive Ease

## The framework

> "Cognitive ease" (Ch 5, line 2124); Figure 5: "Causes and Consequences of Cognitive Ease" (line 2141).

System 1's heuristic: things that are easy to process feel true, familiar, good, and confidence-inspiring. Things that are hard to process feel suspicious, requiring effort, and uncertain.

### The 4 inputs that produce ease

1. **Repetition** — exposure increases ease. ("Frequent repetition is a reliable way to make people believe in falsehoods, because familiarity is not easily distinguished from truth," lines 2231–2234.)
2. **Clear display** — legible font, contrast, simple language.
3. **Priming** — recently activated concepts ease related ones.
4. **Good mood** — positive affect produces ease.

### The 4 outputs ease produces

> "When you are in this comfortable state... you are probably in a good mood, like what you see, believe what you hear, trust your intuitions, and feel that the current situation is comfortably familiar. You are also likely to be relatively casual and superficial in your thinking." (lines 2143–2146)

1. **Judgments of truth** — "the illusion of truth" (line 2231)
2. **Liking / preference** — mere exposure effect
3. **Trust in intuition** — confidence rises
4. **Reduced vigilance** — error detection drops

### The flip: strain produces vigilance

> "When you feel strained, you are more likely to be vigilant and suspicious, invest more effort... make fewer errors, but also... less intuitive and less creative." (lines 2146–2149)

Hard-to-read fonts produce MORE accurate answers on tricky reasoning puzzles. Strain forces System 2 to engage.

### Famous demonstrations

- **Rhyming aphorisms rated more insightful** than non-rhyming versions of the same idea.
- **Easy-to-pronounce names** trusted more (Andrew Klotz vs. Andrjev Krptski).
- **Body temperature of a chicken is 144°** — repeat it three times and people start endorsing it.

## How to apply

When persuading or designing communication:

1. **Reduce strain.** Clear typography, simple language, repetition (within reason), good colors.
2. **Rhyme and rhythm if appropriate** — they boost perceived truth.
3. **Familiar phrases land better than novel ones.**

When auditing your own beliefs:

1. **Ask: do I believe this because it's TRUE or because I've heard it 100 times?**
2. **Deliberately introduce strain on important judgments.** Print arguments in harder fonts. Walk through them slowly. Force System 2 engagement.
3. **Distrust the "feels right" signal on high-stakes calls.** Especially when nothing's been recently challenged.

## Common failure modes

- **Using ease to manipulate.** Repetition produces belief whether or not the underlying claim is true. The framework can be deployed cynically.
- **Confusing ease with quality.** "It's so clear, it must be right" — clarity is a property of the presentation, not the underlying claim.
- **Adding artificial strain on routine decisions.** Most low-stakes tasks should run on System 1. Strain everywhere is exhausting and counterproductive.
