# Kahneman — Anchoring

## The framework

> "Anchoring effect: the assimilation of a numerical estimate toward a previously considered number." (Ch 11)

If a number is mentioned before you estimate something — ANY number — your estimate moves toward it. Even if you know the number is irrelevant. Even if the number was generated by spinning a wheel of fortune in front of you.

### Two mechanisms (System 1 and System 2)

**1. Anchoring as priming (System 1)**
The anchor activates associated information in memory. The mind selectively retrieves data CONSISTENT with the anchor, making the anchor feel plausible.

**2. Anchoring as insufficient adjustment (System 2)**
When asked to estimate, System 2 starts from the anchor and adjusts. It stops adjusting too soon, ending closer to the anchor than it should.

### Demonstrations

- Subjects spun a wheel of fortune that landed on 10 or 65 (visibly random). Then estimated "what % of African nations are in the UN?" Wheel-10 group: avg 25%. Wheel-65 group: avg 45%.
- Judges given a randomly assigned "prosecutor recommendation" sentenced differently — even when they knew it was random.
- Real estate agents shown a house with a listing price 11% higher or lower than market value adjusted their professional appraisals by 41% of the listing-price difference.

### The anchoring index

Most anchoring effects produce a 30–55% pull. So if the anchor is $50 above the "true" value, the estimate ends up $15–28 above where it would have been without the anchor.

## How to apply

When negotiating or estimating:

1. **Set the anchor when you can.** The first number heavily shapes the final range. (Salary negotiation: state your number first if you're well-informed.)
2. **When receiving an anchor you find unreasonable: refuse the frame.** "That number isn't a useful starting point. Let's discuss the basis." If you adjust from it, you've already lost ground.
3. **Counter-anchor with an equally extreme number on the other side.** Two extremes pull the midpoint to roughly fair.
4. **When estimating: deliberately consider an opposite anchor.** "What if the number were 1/10 of this?" The double-consideration reduces (not eliminates) bias.
5. **Set decision rules in advance.** "I will not pay more than X" decided BEFORE entering the negotiation is harder for an anchor to move than a "what feels right" judgment.

## Common failure modes

- **Knowing about anchoring and thinking you're immune.** You're not. Subjects who know about the effect are anchored almost identically.
- **Adjusting "enough."** Subjects asked to consider opposite anchors reduced bias but didn't eliminate it. Structural defenses (pre-commitment, blind procedures) work better than introspection.
- **Treating the first number as a "starting point."** It's a gravitational center. Don't anchor your reasoning around it; anchor your reasoning around the BASIS for valuation.
- **Letting irrelevant numbers in.** "We had a budget of $X for the LAST project" is an anchor for the new project even if the new project is different. So is "our competitor charges Y." Notice and discount.
