# Kahneman / Sibony / Sunstein — Mediating Assessments Protocol (MAP)

## The framework

> "The Mediating Assessments Protocol" (Ch 25, line 10551)
> "A method of decision making in organizations... designed with noise mitigation as a primary objective." (lines 10553–10556)

A structured procedure for complex one-off decisions where the standard alternative is a meeting where executives "discuss and decide." MAP forces decomposition, independence, and structured aggregation — the three principles of decision hygiene applied to a single decision.

### The 6-step procedure

**Step 1: Define the mediating assessments.** (line 10654)
Before any data-gathering, list the 4–10 sub-judgments the decision requires. For an acquisition: market position assessment, technology assessment, team assessment, financial assessment, integration risk, strategic fit, etc.

**Step 2: Assign assessments to different teams independently.** (line 10722)
Different analysts work on different assessments. They don't share notes. Communication minimized.

> "Avoid the halo effect... That's what we are trying to prevent." (line 10727)

If one assessment looks great, you don't want it to color the other teams' assessments. Independence is the protection.

**Step 3: For each assessment, present facts, then collect individual ratings.** (line 10810)
In the decision meeting: assessment 1 is presented (facts only). Board members vote / rate INDIVIDUALLY via app or written ballot BEFORE discussion. Then 2, then 3, etc.

**Step 4: Use estimate-talk-estimate.** (line 10793–10797)
For each assessment:
- Individual silent estimate.
- Brief discussion of reasoning.
- Re-estimate individually after hearing others.

This captures the benefit of discussion (information sharing, reasoning exposure) while preserving independence (no cascade).

**Step 5: Review each assessment separately.** (line 10930)
Do NOT discuss the overall decision while going through assessments. Each assessment gets its own focused review and final group rating before moving to the next.

**Step 6: Delay holistic judgment.** (line 10935)
After all assessments have been individually scored and group-aggregated, THEN — and only then — discuss the holistic decision.

> "To make the final decision, delay intuition, but don't ban it." (line 10935)

The final call CAN be intuitive. But the intuition is now operating on structured, independent, aggregated inputs — not on a halo or cascade.

### Why this works

The standard "executive meeting" pattern: someone presents the deal holistically; senior person responds; cascade. Holistic judgment forms in minute 5. The next two hours are rationalization.

MAP breaks this by:
- Decomposing (preventing holistic rush).
- Independent assignment (preventing halo).
- Individual scoring (preventing cascade).
- Estimate-talk-estimate (preserving learning without losing independence).
- Delayed integration (intuition operates on better inputs).

### The Google example (Ch 24, related)

Google's hiring uses MAP-adjacent structure: 4 mediating assessments (cognitive ability, leadership, googleyness, role knowledge), interviewers score independently on a rubric, only THEN does a hiring committee review the full file and decide. Their structured-interview accuracy is ~65-69% vs. ~56-61% for unstructured.

## How to apply

When facing a major complex decision:

1. **Spend the first meeting on Step 1 only.** What ARE the assessments this decision needs? Get the list right BEFORE gathering data.
2. **Assign each assessment to a separate team or analyst.** Brief them in isolation. They don't see each other's work.
3. **Build the decision-meeting agenda around assessments.** One assessment per agenda item. Each gets: facts presentation → individual rating → discussion → re-rating.
4. **Use a voting tool.** App, written ballot, anything that captures individual scores before anyone talks.
5. **Resist the urge to discuss the holistic decision until all assessments are scored.** People will try; redirect.
6. **At the end: pause.** Look at the assessment scores. THEN have the final discussion. Final decision can be intuitive — but constrained by the structured priors.

## Common failure modes

- **"This is too slow."** It IS slower. The cost is real. The benefit is statistical — across many decisions, accuracy goes up significantly.
- **CEO presents the deal holistically before assessments.** Cascade installed in minute one. The whole procedure becomes theater.
- **Assignments overlap or teams chat informally.** Independence violated; halo effect returns.
- **Skipping estimate-talk-estimate (just discuss).** Lose the independence benefit; back to standard meeting dynamics.
- **Discussing the final decision in the middle of assessment review.** Premature holistic judgment contaminates remaining assessments.
- **Treating MAP as a one-off for very big decisions only.** It's structurally beneficial for moderately complex decisions too. The threshold for adopting it is lower than most teams assume.
