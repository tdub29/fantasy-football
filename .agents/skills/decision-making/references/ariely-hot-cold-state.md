# Dan Ariely — The Influence of Arousal

## The framework

> "The Influence of Arousal." (Ch 5)

When you're calm, you can't accurately predict what your aroused/angry/excited self will do. The "cold state" makes predictions about a "hot state" version of you that turns out wildly wrong.

### The demonstration

Male subjects were asked predictive questions about sexual behavior — in two states:
- **Cold state:** sitting in a lab, thinking calmly. "Would you do X?"
- **Hot state:** during sexual arousal (achieved via private self-stimulation with computer-prompted answers). Same questions.

The hot-state responses showed dramatically higher willingness to engage in behaviors the cold-state self rejected: risky sex, unprotected sex, ethically questionable acts. The same person, asked at different states, gave very different answers.

The cold-state predictions UNDERESTIMATED what hot-state would actually do — by huge margins.

### The generalization

Not just sexual arousal. Any emotionally activated state:
- Anger → say things you "would never" say.
- Fear → decisions you "would never" make.
- Excitement → commitments you "would never" agree to.
- Hunger → food choices you "would never" allow.
- Sleep deprivation → judgments your rested self would reject.

The pattern: cold-state predictions are systematically too rosy. Hot-state behavior is systematically worse.

### Why this is decision-relevant

The cold-state self DESIGNS strategies (diets, communication patterns, financial rules). The hot-state self EXECUTES them. The hot-state self routinely overrides the cold-state plan — and the cold-state self can't predict when.

### The discipline: bind the future self

You can't out-willpower hot-state from inside hot-state. The defense: pre-commitment.

- **Don't have ice cream in the house.** Cold-state self decides; hot-state self can't override (no ice cream available).
- **Auto-deduct savings on payday.** Cold-state self locks in; hot-state self never sees the money.
- **Don't text exes after midnight.** Pre-commitment + delay.
- **Sales: structural sleep-on-it requirements.** Hot-state self can't sign on the spot.

The cold-state self must do the work upfront, knowing that hot-state self will be a different (less reliable) decision-maker.

### The Ulysses pact

Tying yourself to the mast. Ulysses knew that hot-state Ulysses (hearing the Sirens) would order the sailors to release him. Cold-state Ulysses had himself bound BEFORE the temptation arrived. The pre-commitment overrode the hot-state failure mode.

## How to apply

For yourself:

1. **Map your hot-state vulnerabilities.** Anger after work? Hunger before lunch? Excitement at the casino? Fear in negotiations?

2. **For each: design a pre-commitment.** What can cold-state-you do TODAY that makes hot-state-you's bad choices structurally impossible?

3. **Make the pre-commitment costly to escape.** "I'll just try harder next time" doesn't work. The pre-commitment has to bind without willpower.

4. **Don't predict hot-state behavior from cold state.** Especially: don't promise "I would never X." You don't know what hot-you will do. Bind instead.

For organizations:

1. **Crisis playbooks.** Cold-state policy design for hot-state moments (PR crises, layoffs, security breaches). The team-in-crisis cannot reliably design good responses.

2. **Cooling-off periods.** Major decisions require N hours / days of delay before commitment. The hot-state moment can't force immediate signing.

3. **Pre-committed thresholds.** Trading firms have automatic stop-losses for this reason. Hot-state trader would override; algorithm doesn't.

## Common failure modes

- **"I'll just be stronger next time."** Cold-state confidence in willpower. Hot-state will override.
- **Pre-commitments that hot-state can easily escape.** "I'll set my alarm and not snooze" — hot-state snoozes. Need physical or structural binding.
- **Designing for the wrong hot state.** Optimizing for the anger version while the actual hot state is fear, or vice versa.
- **Underestimating which states are "hot."** Mild excitement, slight hunger, low-grade stress — all degrade judgment beyond cold-state expectation.
- **Forgetting that others also have hot/cold states.** Negotiating someone into a "yes" while they're hot doesn't bind them in cold — and trying to enforce hot-state commitments produces resentment.
