# Kahneman / Sibony / Sunstein — Structure in Hiring

## The framework

> "Structure in Hiring." (Ch 24, line 10119)
> "Standard interviews (also called unstructured interviews)... are not very informative. To put it more starkly, they are often useless." (lines 10160–10163)

Unstructured interviews — sit down, have a conversation, come away with a feeling — have correlation with future job performance of around 0.20. That's worse than weighted background checks. Structured interviews push correlation to 0.44–0.57.

> "Structured interviews are far more predictive of future performance than are traditional, unstructured ones." (line 10464)

Three principles produce the gain.

### The three principles

**1. Decomposition** (line 10399)

Pre-define the 4–7 mediating assessments the role requires. Examples (Google):
- General cognitive ability.
- Leadership.
- Cultural fit ("googleyness").
- Role-related knowledge.

What's NOT on the list: looks, charisma, hobbies, interview "performance." If it's not on the list, it doesn't factor.

> "A candidate's good looks, smooth talk, exciting hobbies... are not on the list." (line 10413)

**2. Independence** (line 10432)

Each interviewer evaluates ONE dimension (or a small subset). They score on that dimension only, blind to other interviewers' scores.

> "In the conduct of the interview, they do not evaluate these elements separately. Each assessment influences the others, which makes each assessment very noisy." (lines 10437–10439)

Without independence: interviewer 1 likes the candidate, mentions it; interviewer 2 hears that; all subsequent assessments are halo-affected.

**3. Delayed Holistic Judgment** (line 10478)

The final decision IS made by a hiring committee, AFTER all structured scores are in. They CAN apply judgment. They CAN'T see the candidate before structured scoring is complete.

> "Do not exclude intuition, but delay it." (line 10479)

The committee's intuition operates on structured inputs, not on a charmed first impression.

### The empirical case

> "Correlations with job performance range between .44 and .57." (line 10464)
> "Your chances of picking the better candidate with a structured interview are between 65 and 69%, a marked improvement over the 56 to 61% chance an unstructured interview would give you." (lines 10467–10468)

Structured interviews don't just feel more rigorous — they predict actual outcomes 2-3x better than unstructured equivalents.

### The operational procedure

> "Required interviewers to elicit objective evidence relevant to each dimension in turn and to score that dimension before moving on to the next... allowed recruiters to use judgment and intuition to reach a final decision—but only after the structured evaluation had taken place." (lines 10516–10520)

Step by step:
1. **Pre-define the 4–7 dimensions.** Role-specific. Job analysis informs them.
2. **For each dimension, define what evidence would distinguish high from low.** Behavioral indicators. Specific questions.
3. **Assign dimensions to interviewers.** Each interviewer focuses on their assigned dimensions.
4. **Score immediately after the interview.** Before discussing with other interviewers. Before reading their notes.
5. **Submit scores to a centralized record.**
6. **Hiring committee reviews the structured scores + raw evidence.** Discusses. Makes the final call.

## How to apply

When designing or fixing a hiring process:

1. **Define the dimensions FIRST.** What does success in this role actually require? Decompose. Be specific. 4–7 max.
2. **Build interview questions per dimension.** Behavioral, evidence-eliciting questions. Same questions across candidates.
3. **Assign dimensions to interviewers.** Different interviewers cover different dimensions.
4. **Forbid pre-interview chatter about candidates.** Interviewers form judgments independently.
5. **Score before discussion.** Each interviewer submits structured scores before any cross-talk.
6. **Convene the committee with structured data, not impressions.** The "hire / don't hire" decision happens at the committee level, AFTER scores.

When auditing an existing process:

1. **Look for unstructured interviews.** "Just have a chat with them" = noise generator.
2. **Look for pre-interview chatter.** Does anyone discuss the candidate before scoring? That's halo installed.
3. **Look for decisions made in the room.** If the offer happens right after the last interview, structured scoring wasn't given time to operate.

## Common failure modes

- **"We use structured interviews."** Often means rated 1-5 on vague dimensions, with discussion happening throughout. The structure is decorative. Real structure requires independence + delayed integration.
- **Letting "cultural fit" become a back door.** If "cultural fit" isn't operationalized into concrete behavioral indicators, it becomes "did I like them." The whole point of structure dissolves.
- **One person scoring all dimensions.** Halo within a single rater is real. Different dimensions need different focus and ideally different raters.
- **Letting hiring managers veto without structured input.** "I just don't feel it" overrides scores. That's the unstructured process you were trying to escape.
- **Adding too many dimensions.** 12 dimensions = the rater can't focus; defaults to holistic feel anyway.
- **Skipping the dimension-definition step.** Without explicit dimensions, you have a fancier unstructured interview.
