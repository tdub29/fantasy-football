# Kahneman — System 1 / System 2

## The framework

> "System 1 operates automatically and quickly, with little or no effort and no sense of voluntary control. System 2 allocates attention to the effortful mental activities that demand it." (Ch 1, lines 658–661)

The dual-process model of thinking. Both systems are always operating; the question is which has the floor on a given judgment.

### The two systems

**System 1** — fast, automatic, intuitive, associative, effortless.
- Reads facial expressions, completes idioms, detects threat, drives on empty roads.
- Generates impressions, intuitions, intentions, feelings.
- Can't be turned off. Can't be reasoned with directly. You can only manipulate its inputs.

**System 2** — slow, deliberate, effortful, sequential, controlled.
- Computes 17 × 24, fills in tax forms, focuses attention in noise, checks the validity of an argument.
- Limited capacity (attention is finite). Gets fatigued (ego depletion).
- Lazy by default — most of what feels like "thinking" is System 1 outputs that System 2 has rubber-stamped.

### The division of labor

> "System 1 continuously generates suggestions for System 2: impressions, intuitions, intentions, and feelings. If endorsed by System 2, impressions and intuitions turn into beliefs, and impulses into voluntary actions." (lines 804–806)

System 1 generates; System 2 checks (in theory). The check is often missing — that's where systematic errors come from.

### The conflict

When System 1 and System 2 disagree (Stroop task: the word "RED" written in blue ink), there's effort. System 2 has to override System 1's automatic reading.

> "System 2 is in charge of self-control... it overcomes the impulses of System 1." (lines 887–888)

### Why this is the parent

Every other Kahneman framework — anchoring, availability, representativeness, framing — is a story about System 1 generating an answer that System 2 fails to check. The parent skill: see the system at work.

## How to apply

When examining a judgment (yours or someone else's):

1. **Ask: was this produced by System 1 or System 2?** System 1 outputs feel certain, effortless, and immediate. System 2 outputs are slower, conscious, and explicitly reasoned.
2. **If System 1: what was the input?** Anchor? Cognitive ease? Availability? See references/kahneman-substitution.md for the most common trick.
3. **Was System 2 actually engaged, or just rubber-stamping?** Most "I thought about it" is System 1 output that wasn't verified.
4. **For high-stakes decisions, force System 2.** Write it out. Calculate. Apply a checklist. Outside view. Slow down.
5. **For low-stakes / pattern-recognition tasks, trust System 1.** Most driving, most social reading, most expert pattern-matching. System 2 here is wasteful or counterproductive.

## Common failure modes

- **Treating "intuition" as a single thing.** Some intuitions are decades of pattern recognition (legitimate System 1 expertise). Others are availability/anchoring bias. references/kahneman-outside-view.md for when to trust which.
- **Engaging System 2 then forgetting and reverting.** "I thought about it" two days ago doesn't persist. The intuition that's loud right now is the intuition that wins.
- **Believing you can override System 1 by knowing about it.** Knowing about anchoring doesn't make you immune. You can structurally protect against it (outside view, blind comparisons, formulas) but not by force of will.
