# Dynasty rebuild clock

A rebuild is a schedule, not a mood. It has a start date, a fixed number of selling seasons, a written flip condition, and a termination rule that fires whether or not the condition is met. Anything without those four is a hoard.

## The clock

| Phase | Seasons | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Sell | 1-2 (hard cap 3) | Convert decaying value into youth and firsts |
| Flip | Year 3 or 4 | Reverse pick policy, spend futures on proven starters |
| Payoff | Years 4-8 | The window |

Lag decomposition, why the clock reads this way:
- Pick to startable rookie: 1 year (rookie startability 0.75 in year one).
- Rookie to peak: 2-3 more (RB growTo 24, WR growTo 25, TE 26).
- Contention: 3-4 years after that.

Total: sell-off to window open is 3-4 seasons. State every rebuild plan in these terms or it is not a plan.

## Step 1: sell order

Rank every asset by:

```
sellPriority = dynastyValue x annualValueDecay x (1 - marginalLineupContribution)
```

`marginalLineupContribution` is 0 if removing the player does not change your best legal starting lineup. Those assets sell FIRST, before any core sell, because 100% of their value is trade value decaying for free. In a 1QB league with FLEX limited to RB/WR/TE, QB2 and QB3 are exactly this.

Annual value decay past peak: RB 22%, WR 10%, TE 10%, QB 5%.

Sequence:
1. Zero-lineup-contribution surplus (QB2/QB3 in 1QB, RB6+ in a 4-FLEX lineup).
2. Producers who fail the flip-year age test below, highest value first.
3. Pick classes trading more than 25% over baseline (2027 early firsts are +38.5%).
4. Nothing else. Steps 1-3 are the whole sell list.

## Step 2: flip-year age test

This resolves most hold/sell disputes in one line.

```
SELL unless age + years_to_flip <= holdTo(position)
holdTo: RB 26, WR 28, TE 29, QB 33
```

"Young" is defined relative to the flip year, never to today.

Worked, flipping in 2029 (3 years out):
- Achane, 24 today, arrives at 27. 27 > 26. **SELL**, despite reading young.
- Jeremiyah Love, 21 today, arrives at 24. 24 <= 26 and still growing. **CORE**.
- McBride, 26 today, arrives at 29. 29 <= 29, exactly at the line. **HOLD**, and he is your sell-window asset in 2030-2031, not now.

## Step 3: a rebuild sells AGE, never YOUTH

Selling a 21-year-old RB or a 26-year-old TE restarts the clock the rebuild exists to run down. Young cornerstones are the rebuild's own payoff arriving early. "I'm rebuilding so everything is for sale" is a category error, not aggression.

The sell list is generated by the age curve. Mode does not expand it.

## Step 4: write the flip triggers BEFORE the first sell

Same document as the sell list. Flip when any **2** fire:

1. Value share >= 1/N (10.0% in this 10-team league).
2. >= 4 cornerstones, defined as top-12 dynasty value at position, aged <= 26.
3. Projected starter points top-3 in the projected-year table.
4. Projected title odds >= 12% for the coming season.

On flip: immediately reverse pick policy. Spend futures on proven starters who fit the window. The flip is a switch, not a fade.

Triggers decided after the fact, mid-season, off a 3-1 start, are how the sell phase gets abandoned at 60% completion. That lands you 5th-7th with a mid-first, which is the dominated square.

## Step 5: termination rule

If triggers are unmet at the end of season 3, **flip anyway with the assets on hand.**

A restart converts a 3-year rebuild into a 9-year one and is the most common path to a decade with zero titles. A mediocre flip beats a second sell cycle. This rule is not negotiable by how the assets "feel."

## Step 6: perpetual-rebuild detector

One number: **average age of your top-8 value assets must RISE year over year.**

A working rebuild gets older on purpose as its core matures. Flat average age is a churn loop trading youth for youth, paying the ascending-player premium every offseason for zero expected titles.

Gate: top-8 average age <= 24.5 by end of year 1, rising thereafter.

If age is flat, stop trading maturing assets for younger ones and hold through maturation.

## Step 7: measure progress with leading indicators only

| Indicator | Target |
|---|---|
| Value share | +1.0 to +1.5 points per working offseason |
| Top-8 average age | <= 24.5 end of year 1, rising after |
| Cornerstone count (top-12 at position, age <= 26) | reaching 4+ |
| Projected starter-point rank, year 3-4 table | 8th climbing toward top-3 |

Record carries **zero** information for the first two seasons, and negative information about draft position. Do not report win-loss as rebuild health.

## Timing the market

- **Sell veterans in-season, before the trade deadline.** Contender demand prices producing veterans 10-20% above the offseason baseline.
- **February to April is the rebuilder's BUY window**, not his sell window.

## Buy list while rebuilding

Buy:
1. Post-hype year 2-3 players drafted top-6 in their rookie class whose year-1 shortfall traces to depth chart, not injury and not a doghouse. Pay up to 2x a mid 2nd. You are buying a ~50% hit rate at a ~20% hit-rate price.
2. Injured producers aged <= 24.
3. Talent on bad offenses aged <= 23.

Do not buy:
- Rookies during draft week. That is their annual price peak.
- Aging veterans at a "discount." Value retention goes 0.92 to 0.47 to 0.25 across the sell and hard-sell ages. They will be worth nothing before the window opens.
- Pick classes carrying a >25% hype premium. That is value maintenance, not growth.

## The half-rebuild is strictly dominated

Landing 5th-7th buys essentially no title equity and costs roughly half your first-round pick capital. 1.01 is ~2x the value of 1.05 and ~3.5x that of 1.10. Commit or contend. There is no partial credit.

## Tanking is a weak engine here

6 of 10 make the playoffs, so only 4 teams miss. A deliberately bad roster still makes the playoffs 13-40% of the time by accident, and bottoming out moves you a few draft slots at most.

The engine is trades for young producers plus acquired firsts. Not losing on purpose. Never bench a startable player to chase draft position in this format; the payoff is too small to be worth the roster habit.

## Model both sides of every sell

Selling a producer to the #2 roster raises their title odds and lowers yours by more than the outgoing value suggests. One-sided whatIf systematically overstates the case for selling. Age and convert picks for all 10 rosters, then read your future RANK, not your future points.

## Diagnostic questions

Ask all six before rendering any rebuild verdict. Any unanswered one means the rebuild is not specified well enough to evaluate.

1. What year do you intend to flip?
2. Which of your current top-8 assets pass `age + years_to_flip <= holdTo`?
3. What is your value share versus 1/N?
4. What are the written flip triggers, and who checks them?
5. Which assets have zero marginal lineup contribution today?
6. What season did the sell phase start, and how many selling seasons are left on the cap?

## Worked example: the owner's roster, 2026

State: 8th of 10 in projected starter points, ~1.4% title odds (0.14x fair share), Love 21, Achane 24, McBride 26, five firsts, three startable QBs in a 1QB league.

**Clock set.** Sell seasons 2026 and 2027. Flip offseason before 2029. Payoff 2029-2033. `years_to_flip = 3`.

**Sell order.**
1. QB2 and QB3. Zero marginal lineup contribution. In a 1QB 10-team league QB11 is on waivers, so QB2 is worth ~1.5 expected starts, about 6 points over a season. QB3 is under 1 point. Sell for anything above a 3rd; take a 3rd if that is the market. These go before any core sell.
2. Achane. Fails the flip-year test at 27. He is a producing RB, so he is a DEADLINE sell, not an offseason sell. Which deadline is set by `dynasty-age-curve-timing`: his value peaks at 25, so the sell is the **2027 deadline**, not 2026. Selling at 24 leaves the peak on the table; selling at 26 is late. Waiting past 26 costs 22% of his dynasty value, and waiting until the decline shows in the box score costs 39%.
3. Any 2027 first held past the third. Early 2027 firsts are +38.5% over baseline. Five firsts convert to roughly 2.4 hits and ~1.0 genuine starter-plus, so firsts four and five are bench players with a hype premium attached. Sell them to contenders, buy the post-hype names above.

**Do not sell.** Love (arrives at 24, growing). McBride (arrives at 29, exactly at holdTo). These are the payoff.

**Written flip triggers.** Copy the four from Step 4 into the roster notes today, dated, before the first trade sends.

**Year 1 gate check, end of 2026.** Top-8 average age <= 24.5. Value share up 1.0 to 1.5 points from today. Cornerstone count moving toward 4. If the top-8 average age is flat at the end of 2027, the rebuild has become a churn loop and the fix is to stop trading, not to trade more.

**Termination.** End of 2028, flip regardless. Even a mediocre flip in 2029 beats starting over into 2035.

## Failure modes

1. **The half-rebuild.** Selling two veterans, keeping the rest, landing 6th. No title equity, mid-first, assets a year older.
2. **The perpetual rebuild.** Top-8 average age flat year over year. Churning youth for youth forever, paying the ascending premium every offseason.
3. **Restarting a stalled rebuild.** Triggers unmet at end of season 3, so you sell again. A 3-year plan becomes a 9-year one. This is the single most expensive mistake on the list.
4. **Unwritten flip triggers.** Decided emotionally after a 3-1 start, which is exactly when you are least able to judge.
5. **Selling in the offseason.** Leaves 10-20% of the deadline premium on the table on every veteran sale.
6. **Judging health by record.** Wins carry zero information in seasons 1-2 and negative information about draft slot.
7. **Selling young cornerstones under the banner of rebuilding.** Restarts the clock. A rebuild sells age.
8. **Tanking as the plan.** In a 6-of-10 league the draft-slot payoff is too small to be the engine. Trades are the engine.
9. **One-sided whatIf.** Modeling only your roster after a sell makes every sell look better than it is.
10. **Holding surplus for a market that cannot exist.** A 10-team 1QB league will never bid up your QB3. Take the third-rounder.
