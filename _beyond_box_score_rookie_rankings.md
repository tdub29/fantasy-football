## Top of board — ranks 1–7 NOT visually confirmed (audio inference)

1. **Jeremiah Love** (RB, Cardinals) — Gibbs' 1.01 in both home rookie drafts; Schneier also took him 1.01 ("It went Love-Mendoza or Mendoza-Love"). Best breakaway speed in the class outside of Demond Claiborne. Bell-cow upside. Gibbs frames him in the Chase Brown / Bijan Robinson / Saquon range of fantasy RB ceilings — when ranking Chase Brown RB9 he listed Love just behind Walker as part of that elite tier. In the late-round running back conversation, both hosts repeatedly used "the Jeremiah Love spot" as shorthand for the single elite RB this class has — every other backfield deferred to round 5+ because no one in those rooms drafted "the Jeremiah Love" pick.

2. **Fernando Mendoza** (QB, Raiders) — Jacob: 1.02 Superflex, "don't overthink it." Pairs with Brock Bowers, Ashton Jeanty, and a real OC (Kubiak). Gibbs: "I think it takes a lot of hubris to like pass on that for a receiver." Dan much lower: skeptical the rushing will translate ("I just don't personally feel like he's the athlete he looked like on the Big 10 level"), and in 1QB he drops Mendoza down with Ty Simpson as "basically irrelevant." Gibbs' counter, which won Dan over on air: "you don't have to be some phenomenal athlete to pile up rushing yards. Mahomes isn't a good testing athlete and had crazy amounts of fantasy production as a rusher." Gibbs cites Mendoza's per-dropback scramble rate at Indiana as "basically where Mahomes was in college." Worried only about receiver core; Gibbs is high anyway because of Bowers/Jeanty/Kubiak. Dan's hesitation about Mendoza's rushing was the on-air segment where he conceded ("You sold me on that").

3. **Carnell Tate** (WR, Titans) — Round-one capital, paired with Cam Ward. Dan would take Tate over Lemon: "I debate Lemon as wide receiver 2." Dan calls Tate his "best real-life player of any WR in this class" pick. JJ Zachariason on the same archetype: "I think he could end up being a better real-life football player and NFL'er than any other wide receiver in this class, but I don't know if that translates quite as well to fantasy" — JJ specifically used Tate as "the Carnell Tate dilemma" example for film-vs-model splits. Jacob nervous about leaning on Ward's deep accuracy (Ward's deep accuracy was a weak point in Miami and last year). Intermediate routes should be great with Cam Ward. Tate gets a Tennessee offense that drafted zero offensive help around him after round one (next OL pick was round five). Both hosts flagged Tate could be a redraft-hype trap where he gets pushed up boards based on training-camp video and disappoints in fantasy because Tennessee's OL is still a wreck.

4. **Jordan Tyson** (WR, Saints) — Dan's WR1 of the class for dynasty. Jacob: "perfect spot." Pairs with Tyler Shough and Kellen Moore, with Travis Etienne leading the run game opening up play-action. Per Branch deep-dive, Tyson's best season hit 13.2 yards per target on 10+ air-yard throws, comparable only to Chris Bell (13.3). Saints' luck is unusual: an offensive-minded HC fell to them late, Shough turned out to be the late-round QB hit, and now they layer Tyson, Delp, and Etienne on top. Per JJ Zachariason on Shough as a late-round QB stash: "second-year quarterback, better weapons in New Orleans, love the Kellen Moore offense, showed a rushing floor over three points per game, not alarmingly bad on efficiency."

5. **Makai Lemon** (WR, Eagles) — Jacob debated him as low as WR4 of the round; post-draft coach speak pushed him back up: "the coach speak and the GM like both were really encouraging in the way that they see Lemon… exactly how I see him." Could outscore Tyson or Tate in Year 1 if AJ Brown is traded. JJ Zachariason: pre-draft consensus dynasty WR3 (Mai Lemon was "comfortably ahead of Jordan Addison in a lot of dynasty markets"). 36% target per route run rate in 2025 (second-highest among the class behind Skyler Bell at 36%; Lemon at 30%). Career YPRR ranked 1st in the class on non-screen routes. Career avoided tackle rate above Ja'Marr Chase, A.J. Brown, Ladd McConkey, CeeDee Lamb's range. Risk: if Philly trades AJ Brown, Lemon's redraft hype pushes him too high. Dan: "If the Eagles trade AJ Brown, Lemon could get pushed up boards and be super disappointing." Slot-rate concern: Lemon was mostly used in the slot at USC but was more efficient when working outside.

6. **KC Concepcion** (WR, Browns) — Round-one capital (pick 23). Real target-hog upside; Concepcion was not in the slot very much in 2026 despite being grouped with Cooper Jr. and Lemon as a slot archetype. Mockdraftable comp: near 1:1 athletic comp to Omar Cooper Jr. Career YPRR placed him at the top of this draft's success-rate-vs-zone-and-man group (80%+ vs zone, 70%+ vs man — only Mehki Mews, Cooper Jr., Antonio Williams, and Zachariah Branch joined him in that bucket). Jacob: "easy fantasy-friendly ready-made game. If he can just get a decent quarterback, hopefully next year that happens." Long game because Cleveland's QB room is brutal (Sanders/Gabriel/Pickett). Gibbs notes Concepcion's slot rate dropped to single digits in his final season, signaling he can do more than slot work. Dan: "Concepcion could get pushed up boards and be a super big disappointment."

7. **Omar Cooper Jr.** (WR, Jets) — Career 2.5+ YPRR, 39% avoided tackle rate in 2025 (top of round-one bucket — only CeeDee Lamb at 42% beat him; ahead of Drake London, Quentin Johnston, Terrace Marshall, Kadarius Toney, Rashod Bateman, Malik Nabers, Garrett Wilson, Ja'Marr Chase). Career 2.5 YPRR outside the slot (high A-dot of 13.4); 2.66 YPRR in the slot. 26% target per route run rate — grouped with Zay Flowers, Emeka Egbuka, JSN, Jameson Williams, Rome Odunze, Jordan Addison, Garrett Wilson as the round-one WRs who produced 180+ PPR points despite sub-30% target rates (the analytics episode framed this as the historically more-successful archetype than the high-target group of Nabers/MHJ/Burks/Dotson). 15 career TDs on 15+ air-yard targets — only Chris Olave and Jameson Williams (among 50-ish top-15 picks since 2017) scored TDs at a higher rate on downfield targets. Mockdraftable athletic comp: Casey Concepcion 1-for-1, with Puka Nacua and Emeka Egbuka in 89th-90th percentile range — all 6'0" 200-ish, short arms, big hands (Cooper Jr. 69th percentile hand size). 2024 185 PPR points (3rd-highest individual season in this class behind Skyler Bell and Mai Lemon). Reception Perception (Harmon): really good vs zone, 20th percentile vs press — Gibbs flagged the press release technique as the one clear development area. Year-1 chance if Geno holds up; competes with Mason Taylor, Sadiq, AD Mitchell, and Garrett Wilson for slot work. Gibbs originally moved him down post-draft because of awkward fit with Sadiq, then walked it back — "the more I thought about it, this actually might work… I think Cooper Jr. might be more likely to produce in year one as crazy as that sounds, if the Jets' OL holds up and Geno plays better than last year." Frank Reich's history with slot receivers (Keenan Allen, Michael Pittman, Jordan Matthews, T.Y. Hilton) is favorable to Cooper Jr.'s usage profile. Schneier on the Jets' post-draft presser: "OCJ propaganda was the team going on and on and on about Sadiq and then kind of mentioning Mason Taylor and then 'oh, OCJ is great with the ball in his hands'" — Gibbs flagged that the Jets seem more invested in Sadiq getting routes.

## Ranks 8–36 — confirmed from screenshots

8. **Kenyon Sadiq** (TE, Jets) — Jacob ranks here only because of TEP scoring; in non-TEP Jacob would drop him to just outside round one. Historically unproductive for a round-one TE; Gibbs called him "really raw" with "a clear rawness to his game" and "pretty historically unproductive for a tight end taken in round one." Strengths: blocker, willing in pre-snap motion, can offer something with ball in hands and on downfield targets. Weaknesses: route-running consistency vs zone, third-down reliability, YAC. Jets clearly invested in Sadiq — Gibbs thinks they'll prioritize routes to him over Cooper Jr. Dan: "I'd rather have Stowers regardless of format." Sadiq is one of the players the Frank Reich data set most directly maps onto (ADOT 7-10 yard range).

9. **Ty Simpson** (QB, Rams) — Round-one capital into McVay's offense paired with Puka Nacua. Long-term stash. Dan: cleanest long-term QB stash in the class. In 1QB falls to "where Max Klare is or even lower like below the Emmit Johnson, Claiborne and those types." Gibbs treats Simpson as the archetype example of "just a long-term stash" alongside Klare. In Superflex, Schneier admitted "I'll go Simpson" over Cooper Jr. and Concepcion if you need the QB — explicit implied-odds logic for QB scarcity. Gets the McVay/Puka/loaded-OL/loaded-TE-room context that elevates any QB's outcome distribution.

10. **Jadarian Price** (RB, Seahawks) — Real Year-1 opportunity. Jacob nervous about Price's historical comp profile ("a very thin line that we have to walk with Price for this to work out") but agrees the role is there. Dan slightly higher. Gibbs worries Price isn't a short-yardage rusher and may not pass-block — but Seattle went heavy defense and didn't address the position, so Price could "separate himself" from Helani and Emanuel Wilson. Sharps' late ACL means he's not in the conversation in Year 1. Jacob: "There's a real chance that he kicks the door down out the gate."

11. **Antonio Williams** (WR, Commanders) — Gibbs explicitly said this rank on air. GM/coach-speak post-draft was the most emphatic of any rookie on the board — Schneier reading the full Peters quote on air: "He understands the game, understands zone, understands man, understands how to win… NFL route runner. He can win versus press. He can beat man cover… he doesn't look like a college guy there. He's meticulous with his details. He's a good blocker. He's a willing blocker. He's a tough blocker and he's willing to sell out over the middle of the field. That was a pleasant surprise on the tape on how he might complement Terry McLaurin." 80%+ success rate vs zone, 70%+ vs man (Harmon Reception Perception — same group as Lemon, Concepcion, Cooper Jr., Mehki Mews, Branch). Matt Harmon comp: Sterling Shepard. Pairs with Jayden Daniels. Z + slot complement to McLaurin, can beat press, blocks. Jacob's pick as the most likely day-two-or-later WR to matter for fantasy Year 1: "Antonio Williams, man — he's maybe the biggest winner of any rookie." Dan once Gibbs read the presser: "incredibly compelling case you made there, Jacob… maybe he is the best bet of those guys."

12. **Eli Raridon** (TE, Patriots, round 2) — Jacob's TE2. Same scheme that maximized Gronk; 6'7"–6'8" seam-bender who can "run the seam and bend the seam." Dan: "the last time they drafted a 6'7-6'8 guy who can run the seam and bend the seam was Rob Gronkowski and it was the same offensive scheme. He's not Rob Gronkowski obviously, but he can do similar things from the vertical standpoint up the seam." Pats have publicly said they want him to add weight, so Year-1 likely a Terrance Ferguson-level cameo (Hunter Henry still ahead). Long-term outlook: starter. Gibbs comp on body type: Cole Kmet / Dalton Loveland. Gibbs first noticed him while watching the Notre Dame RBs and Malachi Fields: "he just kept catching my attention. I literally had to go look at my tight end list." Some pre-draft dynasty boards had him at TE12 (Jacob: "he's like the tight end two in the class for me… it will change when we get draft capital").

13. **Oscar Delp** (TE, Saints) — Paired with Kellen Moore. Jacob bets the receiving role will be there. Dan worried Delp may not be a pass-catcher (zero meaningful college production) and may not block: "I just think there's a chance he's just not a pass catcher" and "Delp was like a good blocker for his size more than an actual good blocker." Branch deep-dive: Gibbs spent extensive film time on Delp while watching Branch and Colby Bowman at Georgia — Georgia QB play was "among the worst quarterback play I've watched over the last few years." Could just be the starting in-line TE for the Saints in a Kellen Moore system that historically funnels short targets to TEs and slots. Dan would not bet on him; Gibbs is fine in TEP.

14. **Eli Stowers** (TE, Eagles, round 2) — Jacob's TE3 in TEP; highest hypothetical upside. **Dan ranks Stowers TE1 of the class** — "by far the best receiver of these tight ends." In non-TEP Jacob would push Stowers above Sadiq to TE2. Dan: "Stowers to me is so good movement-skills-wise and as a receiver that he has something he can definitely do for an NFL team. I'm not as sure about that with Delp and Raridan." Pushes him into round-one if AJ Brown is traded. Risk: a deep depth chart in Philly may bury him; "Stowers could look really good in camp and then just not get the targets."

15. **Chris Bell** (WR, Dolphins) — Round-two upside swing. Jacob loves the player and situation; sees a 20–30% chance of a late-season Year-1 breakout. Big physical winner. 13.3 yards per target on 10+ air-yard throws (best single season of any non-Tyson receiver in this class). Top of the class in yards per target on receptions where he avoided a tackle (group included Concepcion, Hurst, Cooper Jr., Lemon, Fields — Gibbs called them "dogs at the catch point" with strong ball-carrier upside). Dan's upside-swing cluster names him alongside Hurst/Williams/Stribbling in the post-tier-1 "fourish range." Dan: "one of the few in this group who could legitimately become a #1 NFL WR (with Hurst)."

16. **Brenen Thompson** (WR, Chargers) — Jacob's conviction-call sleeper. "Tank Dell is one of one — don't ever compare anyone to Tank Dell — but it's like this guy kind of might be like that. Like he is awesome for somebody who's so small." Paired with Justin Herbert; long-term bet. Joined Gibbs' top-8 receiver board late after love-at-first-watch on film. Dan likes the player but won't push him this high — too crowded with Mookie Betts (i.e. Quentin Johnston), Tre Harris, Ladd McConkey: "I would never have Brennan Thompson that high. I love him… I just don't see that much of a path for fantasy success there personally." Gibbs: "he had a really stripped-down route tree in college and probably needs to work on refinement." Dan does think Thompson is the kind of move that "opens up space" for Quentin Johnston and Tre Harris and helps Justin Herbert overall.

17. **Max Klare** (TE, Rams) — Wait-and-see TE; Jacob feels strongly about the profile. Dan thinks he gets buried in a deep room: "Max Klare I would never draft because I think he's going to get buried personally in that — it's a deep tight end room and he's going [to be a good real-life player]." Gibbs uses Klare as the archetype of "the long-term stash you're not getting much out of in year 1."

18. **Denzel Boston** (WR, Browns) — Round-two capital. Jacob *lower than consensus* — worries Boston doesn't see the field over Tillman/Concepcion/Fannin, and once he's buried in Cleveland's QB mess the path closes: "if we're not able to make an evaluation about him this year, then I don't really see him as much different than guys like Ted Hurst or Chris Bell or Brennen Thompson, and their situations feel a lot better than being buried in Cleveland." Pairs with a 21-year-old Harold Fannin and Concepcion who can both be legit target hogs. Dan more bullish; thinks Boston plays ahead of Tillman: "I think Boston's a better player than Tillman… he's a better prospect. I feel like Boston's a better player than him." Gibbs: "Concepcion is going to push Tilman down the depth chart, and I just don't trust that Boston actually plays ahead of Tilman." Gibbs concerned Boston "kind of struggled at times" against higher levels of competition.

19. **Ted Hurst** (WR, Buccaneers) — Both hosts in love. Schneier wanted the Giants to draft him instead of Malachi Fields: "I wanted Ted Hurst." Dan's upside-swing tier puts him 4th: "Stribbling, Antonio Williams, Hurst, and Bell in that fourish range." Dan: Hurst is one of the few in this group who could legitimately become a #1 NFL WR (with Chris Bell). Matt Harmon comp: Tory Horton-style mover (movement only, not the player) — Gibbs careful to note Harmon's framing. Per JJ Zachariason's strength-of-schedule adjustment: small-school discount applies (Georgia State) but "there's a lot of intrigue to his size and stuff like that as well… in this class, especially within a weaker class, I'm like, yeah, you can throw that dart." JJ's framework: Hurst's numbers need to look "that much better" to be on equal footing with FBS WRs, but the size + intrigue clears the bar within this weaker class. Gibbs flagged that Hurst's price *won't* drop the way Thompson/Klare/Delp/Raridon will — "Hurst maybe not so much" on the late-round value drop, so pay sticker. Top of the class in yards per target on receptions where he avoided a tackle. Both hosts treat him as a long-term stash but Dan is the higher-conviction buyer. Tampa fit: Gibbs "love the Tampa fit. Love it."

20. **Zachariah Branch** (WR, Falcons, round 3, pick 15 of round 3 — 12th WR off the board) — Jacob came around on the film. Better hands and ball tracking than the "screen gadget" rep suggested. 2.68 YPRR in 2025, 13% first-down-per-route-run rate, 20% explosive reception rate on a brutal 3.6-yard ADOT (literally the lowest in the class). 93 targets, 81 catches, 811 yards, 6 TDs at Georgia behind brutal QB play. Reception Perception: 80%+ vs zone, 70%+ vs man (joined Mehki Mews, Concepcion, Cooper Jr., Antonio Williams in that cluster). 23% career avoided tackle rate (above Rashee Rice, Zay Flowers, Concepcion; just below Ja'Marr Chase, A.J. Brown, Lemon, Ladd McConkey, CeeDee Lamb). Caveat from Gibbs: Branch ranked 108th of 111 day-1/2 receivers since 2017 in yards-per-target on plays with an avoided tackle — "his avoided tackle rate is kind of inflated because he was often avoiding multiple tackles on plays where it was obvious the screen was going to him." Mock Draftable athletic comp #1: Zay Flowers (84% similarity). Other comps in the list: Mecole Hardman, Dwayne Eskridge, Anthony Gould, Trey Tucker, Malik Washington. Body comp: 177 lb, 9th percentile wingspan, 3rd percentile arm length. Branch's screen-target profile mapped most similarly to Rashee Rice's college screen profile. Removing screens: only Jeremy Bernard had a lower non-screen YPRR among day-1/2 WRs — but Branch was still serviceable at 2.1 YPRR on 235 non-screen routes with a 28% explosive reception rate (only behind Lemon and Chris Brazell). On 10+ air-yard targets in 2025: 14 of 21 for 13.0 yards per reception (just below Bell's 13.3 and Tyson's 13.2). Best Year-1 contribution bet of this range — wide-open slot role behind London/Pitts, Tua/Stefanski's offense funnels short targets to slot/TE. Gibbs: "I'm intrigued… much more exciting version of this that I feel a lot more confident in is Omar Cooper Jr."

21. **Jonah Coleman** (RB, Broncos) — Skill set is real, could eventually take work from RJ Harvey, but Sean Payton's offense hasn't blocked the run well since he got to Denver. Coleman had the 4th-highest explosive run rate in the class (Gibbs: "I don't understand why people act like he's not explosive. I think it's just because he's stout"). Probably a Year-2 thing. Dan would rank Demond Claiborne and Emmit Johnson ahead. Dan on Coleman vs Harvey: "I liked Harvey's tape better than Coleman's. I thought Harvey offers more at the NFL level." Dan would put Emmit Johnson over Coleman: "I might argue Emmit over Coleman because I don't see the Chiefs ever being like 'all right, we signed Walker to be a workhorse.'"

22. **Germie Bernard** (WR, Steelers, round 2) — Jacob: "I think he actually might be a really good NFL player." Year-1 chance only on injury or QB disaster. Dan loves the player but caps price because of Pittsburgh's QB room ("you need Aaron Rodgers to come back… if it's Will Howard or Drew Allar, it's really bad"). Dan retracted his own pick of Bernard as a Year-1 contributor on air: "I'm actually completely out on Bernard in year one. This is a bad call. I'm mad I said it." Bernard was the WR with the LOWEST non-screen YPRR among day-1/2 receivers — but he's also the player Pittsburgh used a round-two pick on as their starting slot. Pittsburgh's OL is the saving grace; Mike Tomlin/McCarthy era.

23. **Malachi Fields** (WR, Giants) — Jacob: "really underrated as a mover… not Nico Collins, but more twitch than people credit him for." Possibly best blocking receiver in the class at 6'4". Dan came around on Fields post-draft after watching the Virginia tape Gibbs sent him: "Fields is really good… he's actually really good football player. I think he can help you guys." Dan now considers Fields as a power-slot mismatch piece who could pair with Likely. Gibbs framing: "could be the best blocking receiver in this class at 6'4" with the upside to be." Schneier framing: "if they really just view him as a power slot, you have such an advantage from a run blocking standpoint." Could contribute Year 1 in a Giants offense without an obvious #2 target.

24. **De'Zhaun Stribbling** (WR, 49ers, round 2) — Dan would rank as high as round-two range: "Stribbling for me would be right after Stowers at the lowest." Shanahan-system fit who can sell vertical, snap inside, with 4.37 breakaway speed. Dan: "I think Stribbling can do that really, really well and then has like some underrated breakaway speed on tape." Jacob lower because of age (24) and limited route-winning upside; doesn't expect him to play unless Pearsall gets hurt — "I don't think he's going to play unless there's an injury." 33rd-overall draft capital. Gibbs concern: produced too late in career (only his final season at WSU/Mississippi State), small chance he's ever a top-2 target on a team. Dan's counter: "I'm betting on the draft capital, I'm betting on the role they find from him, I'm betting on the offense that works in San Fran." Gibbs has a clear concern about San Francisco's late-round skill-position eval track record: "they've kind of missed not just the running back but basically any skill position players that they've been drafting lately outside of round one."

25. **Demond Claiborne** (RB, Vikings) — Jacob much higher than consensus. "I'd rather take a shot on Claiborne than Emmit Johnson, Mike Washington, Nicholas Singleton, Katron Allen." 4.37 speed at 188 lbs, best breakaway threat outside of Jeremiah Love. Two 96+ yard KR TDs on just 22 college returns (1 of every 11 returns went for a 96+ yard score). 5'10" 195. Career rates: nearly 5 yards per carry over the last two years, almost 2,000 rush yards, 21 TDs. 55 career receptions, 429 receiving yards (but only 1.0 YPRR — concerning). Dan comp: Travis Etienne style (patient, upright, stop-start, then bursts through). Five fumbles in 2025 — Dan worried about that. BMI under 27 (along with Heidenreich) — historically only CJ2K and CJ Spiller hit at that BMI since 2000 (per Brian Heath research). Schneier on Claiborne (pre-draft sleeper #6): "outside of Jeremiah Love, he has the best breakaway speed in this class… stop-start ability, patient, one of the most patient backs in the class." Dan also flagged Claiborne's background as a motivational factor (rural Virginia, single mom, first in his community to leave the county). Kevin O'Connell's offensive DNA loves an explosive change-of-pace back — "they tried to find something like that at one point with Ty Chandler. This is a much better version of what that can offer."

26. **Kaleb Black** (RB, 49ers) — Day-two RB on a great offense; CMC insurance. Dan worried Lynch/Shanahan have a bad RB-eval track record: "the only time he's ever hit at running back is when he traded for Christian McCaffrey." Jordan Mason was a UDFA hit who got traded. Gibbs fine with the bet: "I'd rather just have Black than those guys… if Christian McCaffrey goes out and he does get the role, that's so much more meaningful than anything else here." Dan, on whether Black moves ahead of Emmit Johnson: "It's tough, man. It's really tough. He's my next up just because their path is so much clearer. CMC wears down, they start to take him off the field."

27. **Justin Joly** (TE, Broncos) — Late-round TE stash in Sean Payton's offense. Fits Jacob's "stash several TEs in the 30–50 range" thesis.

28. **Elijah Sarratt** (WR, Ravens, round 4) — Dan's pre-draft conviction call. "If you're going to see a Keenan Allen type hit in this draft, it would be Sarratt for me." Ravens GM hyped him publicly as a power-slot. Dan would have him in round two; Jacob pushed lower because of the crowded Baltimore room (Bateman, Flowers, Adam Randall) and uncertainty about the new offensive coordinator. Gibbs: "they added some new pieces… don't really know exactly what the role will be — but maybe Sarratt could be 'like Burden at' it." Sarratt was Cooper Jr.'s wide receiver teammate at Indiana — technically more dependable in Indiana's playoff push than OCJ. Got round-four capital despite that.

29. **Skyler Bell** (WR, Bills, round 4) — Dan added post-draft: more NFL-ready than several names above, but "Khalil Shakir is just the better version of him in that offense." Bell had the highest target-per-route-run rate of any WR in the class at 36%. Older prospect — Gibbs warns "you don't want to overreact to him dominating younger competition" — but the breakout year was huge.

30. **Bryce Lance** (WR, Saints) — Trey Lance's brother. Small-school sleeper archetype paired with Hurst in BBS's "classic sleeper" bucket. JJ Zachariason's age/strength-of-schedule warning specifically calls out Lance: "Bryce Lance played two additional seasons and he was older when he was running his routes, doing it at a smaller school. So it's not an apples-to-apples comparison to throw Concepcion in the same bucket as Bryce Lance."

31. **Taylen Green** (QB, Browns) — Dan's pre-draft sleeper #8. The "implied odds" pick — Dan dedicated several minutes of the sleepers show explaining poker implied-odds theory to argue this is the right way to think about a QB who probably never starts: "logic would tell you should never make this call, but the implied odds tell you it's an automatic call because the implied odds say the 16% of the time you do hit, this guy's playing his kings or aces face up." 4.37 at 6'6", 239 lbs, 11'2" broad jump. "How many quarterbacks have we ever seen run a 4.37 at 6'6" 239 with an 11'2" broad jump? None is the answer." Almost certainly never starts behind Sanders/Gabriel/Dillon, but if he ever does he's a top-10 fantasy QB on rushing alone. Comp Schneier offered: Jalen Milroe-style late-round implied-odds QB pick from last cycle, plus the Anthony Richardson "when he started games he was good for you" template. Implicit nickname suggestion from Gibbs: "He's the implied odds pick of this class."

32. **Carson Beck** (QB, Cardinals) — Dan: "Mike Glennon without the arm talent." Explicit do-not-draft from Dan: "I would personally not rank Carson Beck on my board. He's an undraftable grade… I view him in the sense of like when the Jets drafted Christian Hackenberg, this has no chance to work." Jacob took him anyway for the implied odds: "in superflex leagues, you can trade a 48-year-old Philip Rivers for a fourth-round rookie pick just because he's going to start a game."

33. **Sam Roush** (TE, Bears) — Late-round TE stash. Mentioned in Jacob's "stash three or four TEs and one might hit" thesis. The Roush pick was BBS's signal that Chicago is committing to heavy 12/13 personnel — which Jacob argued could actually concentrate targets onto Loveland + one of Odunze/Burden. Schneier counter: "I think this Roush pick could end up hurting Loveland in some ways as annoying as that might be."

34. **Jack Endries** (TE, Bengals, round 7) — Jacob's "seal of approval" — "go watch him vs Ohio State, the blocking pops." Cincinnati fit is good. Round-two-to-three in TEP at fair value. Jacob: "I thought he could have gone a lot earlier than that. Young tight end. I liked him a lot." Endries got bumped over Schneier's Bucky-Irving-bench-press tests because Schneier hasn't watched the film yet. The Bengals also signed Jack Dingle as UDFA, suggesting Erick All may not be safe.

## Bakery / Analytics callouts

These are specific BBS-cited model/analytics claims worth preserving.

- **Cooper Jr.'s 39% avoided tackle rate** is the highest single-season round-one-WR avoided tackle rate since CeeDee Lamb's 42% — only Lamb is above him in the comparable window.
- **Cooper Jr.'s 15 career TDs on 15+ air-yard targets** — only Chris Olave and Jameson Williams (among top-15 picks since 2017) scored TDs at a higher rate on downfield targets.
- **5 receivers from this class** had a success rate >80% vs zone AND >70% vs man (per Harmon Reception Perception): Mehki Mews, Casey Concepcion, Omar Cooper Jr., Antonio Williams, Zachariah Branch.
- **Round-one WRs picked 17-32 with career slot rate >50%** since 2017: Justin Jefferson, JSN, Emeka Egbuka, Jordan Addison, Ricky Pearsall, Treylon Burks, Kadarius Toney — and this class's Lemon, Concepcion, Cooper Jr. all three.
- **Round-one WRs with sub-30% target rates who put up 180+ PPR points in a season**: OCJ, Zay Flowers, Egbuka, Tutu Atwell, JSN, Jameson Williams, Rome Odunze, Jordan Addison, Garrett Wilson. The high-target counter-group (Nabers, MHJ, Dotson, Burks) has been less consistently successful as pros (Nabers the obvious exception).
- **JJ Zachariason's ZAT model: Jordan Addison is the major historical hit signal in this class**: 99th-percentile breakout score (95) — a tier that includes Mike Evans, Sammy Watkins, Amari Cooper, Corey Davis, CeeDee Lamb, Ja'Marr Chase, Chris Olave, Jonathan Mingo, Zay Flowers, Malik Nabers. JJ has Addison at #102 overall in Superflex this year (his rookie pick #2 ahead of Mendoza). Pre-draft consensus dynasty WR3, sliding to WR4-5 in some markets. JJ: "I'm 1 million percent — the model has been right more often than me going away from it."
- **JJ's tight end model revamp** layers draft capital + athleticism + age-adjusted production. The Hunter Henry vs Michael Mayer comparison (same draft slot, same production, Mayer the better athlete — yet model preferred Henry) shows the layered effect of draft capital.
- **JJ's breakout score for WR**: age and schedule-adjusted receiving yards per team pass attempt. Just as predictive as draft capital itself for top-100 picks; day-three becomes the "wild west."
- **JJ's running back model is heavily reception-based** — naturally biased toward Eli Heidenreich because of his crazy receiving profile. Heidenreich is the model's late-round bet but "more of a have-fun-with-it last-round kind of pick."
- **Pre-draft, JJ's RB2 was Emmit Johnson**. Post-draft he's still positively rated by the model but the day-three capital significantly compresses what the gap was.
- **Demond Claiborne and Eli Heidenreich** both have a BMI in the second percentile for RBs (Heidenreich 26.9%, Claiborne 27%). Per Brian Heath / James Foster, the only two backs to hit at BMIs under 27-25 since 2000 are Chris Johnson (CJ2K) and CJ Spiller.
- **Emmit Johnson** led FBS with 870 rushing yards through A/B gaps, had the 5th-highest success rate vs Power-4 (53.4%), and averaged 6.4 YPC on inside runs vs stacked boxes. Pass-pro grade 42.6 was the red flag.
- **Reception Perception (Harmon)**: Cooper Jr. ahead of Quentin Johnston and Kadarius Toney; "really good vs zone" but 20th percentile vs press.
- **JJ on Skyler Bell**: "He's not going to put up the raw numbers he puts up last year in most other places. You just have to be realistic about it… but you can squint and see some outcome where this could work out." Same framing applies to Ted Hurst — strength-of-schedule discount applied, but in this weaker class "you can throw that dart."
- **Streaming QB Frankenstein hit rates**: 2015-2020 = QB6-QB8 in PPG. 2021-2025 = QB11-QB12. Market sharpening means the mobile-QB-late strategy has lost edge, but the baseline matters because "the position is really really deep this year."
