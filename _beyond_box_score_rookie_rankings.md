# Beyond the Box Score — 2026 Rookie Rankings

Hosts: Jacob Gibbs and Dan Schneier (CBS Sports). Scope: 2026 NFL Draft rookies, dynasty unless noted. Synthesized from BBS YouTube transcripts, captured 2026-05-22.

Numbered ranks below are Jacob's explicit post-draft Superflex + Tight End Premium board (top 30 he read on air). Dan agreed on the top 5 but differs on tight ends and several day-three running backs; disagreements are flagged in line. After the top 30 BBS stops giving ordered ranks and starts grouping by archetype, so the back half is grouped that way.

## Tier 1 — The locks (picks 1.01-1.04)

1. **Jeremiah Love** (RB, drafted day one) — 1.01. Jacob and Dan both took him at the top of their home rookie drafts. Best breakaway speed in the class outside of Demond Claiborne, full bell-cow upside.
2. **Fernando Mendoza** (QB, Raiders, 1st round) — 1.02 Superflex. Paired with Brock Bowers, Ashton Genty, and a real OC. Jacob: don't overthink it, take the QB1 of the class. Dan: lower than Jacob on Mendoza, skeptical the rushing translates, would push him into the 5-6 range; in 1QB formats Dan drops him alongside Ty Simpson as basically irrelevant.
3. **Carnell Tate** (WR, Titans) — Round-one draft capital, paired with Cam Ward. Jacob nervous about relying on Ward's deep accuracy but ranks him third because of the capital. Dan would take Tate over Mai Lemon. He's also Dan's "better real-life player than any WR in this class" pick.
4. **Jordan Tyson** (WR, Saints) — Perfect spot. Pairs with Tyler Shough and Kell Moore, ETN-led run game opens it up. Dan calls Tyson his WR1 in the class for dynasty.

## Tier 2 — Round one talent, real questions (picks 1.05-1.11)

5. **Mai Lemon / Rakim Lemon** (WR, Eagles) — Jacob debated him as low as WR4. Coach speak post-draft matched what the film showed and pushed him back up. Could outscore Tyson or Tate. Risk: if Philly trades AJ Brown, Lemon's redraft hype gets him pushed up boards and he becomes a likely fantasy disappointment.
6. **Casey Concepcion / Conpsion** (WR, Browns) — Round one. Real target-hog upside if Cleveland's QB situation stabilizes. The QB room (Shedeur Sanders, Gabriel, possibly DeShaun-something) is brutal so it's a long game.
7. **Omar Cooper Jr.** (WR, Jets) — Round one slot/flex. Career 2.5+ yards per route run, 39% avoided tackle rate (top of the round-one bucket, ahead of CeeDee Lamb's range). Jacob warmed up to the Jets fit after originally souring; Cooper Jr. profiles as a Puka/Egbuka/Concepcion-style 6'0", short-arm, big-hands inside-out winner. Real chance to contribute Year 1 if Geno stays upright; competes with Mason Taylor and Sadiq for slot/middle work.
8. **Kenyan Sadique / Kony Sadeeq** (TE, Jets) — Jacob ranks here only because of TEP scoring. He's "raw," historically unproductive for a round-one TE, but the Jets are invested. Dan ranks Sadique behind Eli Stowers regardless of format.
9. **Ty Simpson** (QB, Rams) — Round-one capital into McVay's offense with Puka and the tight ends. Long-term stash. Dan: probably the cleanest pure long-term QB stash in the class. In 1QB he falls into the round-three range.
10. **Jadarian Price** (RB, Seahawks) — Round one. Real Year-1 opportunity. Jacob is nervous about the historical comp profile but says the role will be there. Dan slightly higher than Jacob.
11. **Antonio Williams** (WR, Commanders) — Round one. The GM/coach-speak post-draft was unusually emphatic — they see him as a Z + slot complement to Terry McLaurin who can also beat press and block. Plays with Jayden Daniels. Jacob's pick as the most likely day-two-or-later WR to actually matter for fantasy Year 1.

## Tier 3 — Round two upside, mostly long-term bets (picks 1.12-2.06 area)

12. **Eli Riden / Eli Raridan** (TE, Patriots, round 2) — Jacob's TE2. Same offensive scheme that maximized Gronk; can bend the seam at 6'7"-6'8". Pats want him to add weight, so Year 1 likely a Terrance Ferguson-level rookie cameo, but the long-term outlook is a starting TE.
13. **Oscar Delp** (TE, Saints) — Paired with Kell Moore. Jacob bets on the receiving role being there. Dan worried Delp may simply not be a pass-catcher (zero college production) and may not even be a good NFL blocker.
14. **Eli Stowers / Eli Sters** (TE, Eagles, round 2) — Jacob's TE3 in TEP, but the highest hypothetical upside. Dan ranks Stowers as TE1 in the class — by far the best receiver of these tight ends — and pushes him up into the round-one range if AJ Brown is traded.
15. **Chris Bell** (WR, Dolphins) — Round two upside swing. Jacob loves the player and the situation, sees a 20-30% chance of a late-season Year-1 breakout. Big physical winner.
16. **Brennan Thompson** (WR, Chargers) — Jacob's conviction-call sleeper. "Tank Dell is one of one, but this guy kind of might be like that." Long-term bet paired with Herbert. Dan likes the player but won't push him this high; sees the role as too crowded with Mookie, Quentin Johnston, Tre Harris.
17. **Max Claire / Max Klare** (TE, ?) — Wait-and-see TE, Jacob feels strongly about the profile. Dan thinks he gets buried in a deep TE room.
18. **Denzel Boston** (WR, Browns) — Round-two capital. Jacob is *lower than consensus* — worries Boston doesn't see the field over Tilman/Conpsion/Fannin, and once you bury him in Cleveland's QB mess the path to mattering closes. Dan more bullish than Jacob on Boston; thinks he plays ahead of Tilman.
19. **Ted Hurst** (WR, ?) — Classic Jacob/Dan sleeper archetype, smaller-school upside. Movement comp on the show was a Tory Horton type.
20. **Zachariah Branch** (WR, Falcons) — Jacob came around on Branch after watching tape. Better hands and ball tracking than the "screen gadget" reputation suggested. 80%+ vs zone, 70%+ vs man on reception perception. Best Year-1 contribution bet from this range — wide-open slot role behind Drake London / Kyle Pitts in a Kevin Stefanski offense that funnels short targets to the slot/TE.
21. **Jonah Coleman** (RB, Broncos, round 3-4) — Skill set is real and could take work from RJ Harvey eventually, but Sean Payton offense hasn't blocked the run well. Probably a Year-2 thing. Dan would put Demond Claiborne and Emmit Johnson ahead of Coleman.
22. **De'Shawn Stribbling** (WR, 49ers, round 2) — Dan would rank him as early as the round-two range — sees him as a Shanahan-system fit who can sell vertical and snap inside, with breakaway speed people are sleeping on. Jacob keeps him lower because of age (24) and limited route-winning upside; doesn't expect him to play unless Pearsall gets hurt.
23. **Malachi Fields** (WR, ?) — Jacob: "I think he's really underrated as a mover... not Nico Collins, but more twitch than people credit him for." Could contribute Year 1 in the right situation.
24. **Jeremy Bernard** (WR, Steelers, round 2) — Jacob: "I think he actually might be a really good NFL player." Could be on the field Year 1 if there's an injury. Dan loves the player but caps the price because of Pittsburgh's QB room (Rudolph / Will Howard / Drew Allar).
25. **Elijah Sorat / Elijah Surratt** (WR, Ravens) — Dan's pre-draft conviction call. "If you're going to see a Keenan Allen type hit in this draft, it would be Sorat for me." Power-slot upside per Ravens GM. Dan would have him in round two; Jacob pushed him into the round-three range only because of the crowded Baltimore room (Bateman, Flowers, plus new pass-catching RB Adam Randall) and uncertain Year-1 role.
26. **Kaylin Black** (RB, 49ers, round 3) — Day-two RB on a great offense. McCaffrey eventually wears down. Dan worried Lynch/Shanahan have a bad RB-eval track record (Mason was the only hit, and they traded him). Jacob fine with the bet.
27. **Emmit Johnson** (RB, Chiefs, round 5) — JJ Zachariason's RB2 pre-draft per his model. Dan agrees and pushes him *ahead of* Jonah Coleman — sees a Kareem Hunt-style path to a 1B role in KC behind Walker. Jacob lower; thinks Johnson may not even be active on game day given Brashard Smith and Demarcado.
28. **Bashar Tuten / Bhayshul Tuten** (RB, Jaguars) — Listed as a 2026-season "winner" rather than a numbered rookie pick — clear lead-back path in Liam Coen's scheme, especially after Jacksonville drafted blocking tight end Kirchshire and guard Brignon. Dan and Jacob both bullish but split on Tuten vs Chris Rodriguez ("the answer might be yes, both").
29. **Demond Claiborne / Demond Claybourne** (RB, Vikings backfield) — Jacob is much higher than consensus. "I'd rather take a shot on Claiborne than Emmit Johnson, Mike Washington, Nick Singleton, Katron Allen." 4.37 speed, best breakaway threat outside of Love.
30. **Kobe Young** (WR, Bengals, round 4) — Bengals had been searching for the Germaine Burton archetype. Late-round, low-cost shot at a bigger explosive receiver.

## The 10 Favorite Sleepers (from biTfw8PvsM0, in BBS's stated order)

This list was pre-draft (2026-04-17), so verdicts are about the player, not the eventual landing spot.

1. **Nicholas Singleton** (RB, Penn State → Titans, round 5) — Dan. Hated by the community; Dan loves the receiving profile (102 career rec, 1,000+ rec yards), pass-pro, zero career fumbles, burst, 54 college TDs. "Singleton way down the board right now in fantasy analysts' minds, and I love that."
2. **John Michael Gillenborg / JMG** (TE, Wyoming) — Jacob. Big, smooth-moving, 6'6"/249 with short arms and small-school production. "Per route run rate basically the best of any tight end in this class." Hipster pick (FantasyPros #92). Landed in **Kansas City as a UDFA** — Jacob is now treating him as the Kelce heir-apparent.
3. **Anthony Johnson** (RB, Nebraska) — Dan. "If there were a Bucky Irving in this class, the style John Johnson runs with is what it reminds me of." 4.55 forty tanked his stock, but the foot quickness, shin angles, and 66 forced missed tackles are real. Pass-pro is a concern. Comp: Singletary with more juice.
4. **Eli Heidenreich** (RB/WR weapon, Navy) — Dan. 6.55 three-cone — better than CMC, JSN, Edelman. Total mismatch weapon if a Shanahan/McVay/Kubiak-type coach scoops him. Sub-30-inch arms and no real route tree are the red flags. Fun late-round name to know.
5. **Ja'Tavion Sanders** (TE, Texas, formerly Cal) — Jacob. "Blocks his freaking butt off" — won reps vs Arvel Reese, pinned Sunny Styles, bowled Caleb Downs. 2.32 yards per route run at Cal. Will be basically free in drafts and might end up a starting TE.
6. **Demond Claiborne** (RB, Wake Forest → ?) — Dan. 4.37 speed, best breakaway in class outside of Love. Two 96+ yard KR TDs on 22 returns. Comp: Travis Etienne style. Underrated as a receiver (55 career rec). Jacob: better than Keaton Mitchell, similar mold.
7. **Kalen / Kaylon Ballage** (RB, Indiana → ?) — Jacob. "Discount Jonah Coleman." Old (4 years at JMU before Indiana), no receiving profile, but he's a dog blocker and was more efficient in short yardage than starter Roman Hemby. Late-pick lottery ticket like Kyle Monangai.
8. **Taylen Green** (QB, Arkansas → ?) — Dan. The "implied odds" pick. 4.37 at 6'6", 239 lbs, 11'2" broad jump. Bad current passer, but if he ever starts, he's a top-10 fantasy QB on rushing alone. Cost is nothing.
9. **"All the freaking tight ends"** — Jacob. Stash multiple TEs in the 30-50 range of dynasty boards: Justin Shockey, Michael Trigg, Tanner Koziol, Sam Roush, Jack Andreas, Oscar Delp, Eli Stowers, Eli Riden, Max Claire, Marlon Klein. TE volatility means even one hit returns far more than the cost.
10. **"All the freaking receivers"** — Jacob. After the top 5 WRs, the WR6-25 range is full of intriguing bets. Named: Chris Bell, Skyler Bell, Jacobi Lane, Malachi Fields, Antonio Williams, Ted Hurst, Bryce Lance, Deion Burks, Brennan Thompson, Dezmond Stripling, Elijah Sorat.
