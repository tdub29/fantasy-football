# Kahneman — Answering an Easier Question (Substitution)

## The framework

> "Substitution... the operation of answering one question in place of another." (Ch 9, lines 3530–3531)

System 1's most pervasive trick: when the target question is hard, it silently swaps in an easier heuristic question, answers THAT, and reports the answer as if you'd answered the original.

> "If a satisfactory answer to a hard question is not found quickly, System 1 will find a related question that is easier and will answer it." (lines 3529–3530)

### The substitution table (Ch 9, lines 3574–3593)

| Target question (hard) | Heuristic question (easy) |
|---|---|
| How much would you contribute to save an endangered species? | How much emotion do I feel about dying dolphins? |
| How happy are you with your life these days? | What is my mood right now? |
| How popular will the president be in 6 months? | How popular is the president right now? |
| How should financial fraudsters be punished? | How much anger do I feel at financial fraudsters? |

The substitution is automatic and invisible. The output FEELS like an answer to the target.

### Three named sub-mechanisms

**1. Intensity matching**
> "Both feelings and contribution dollars are intensity scales... The dollar amount that will come to my mind is the matching amount." (lines 3604–3608)

Your emotion has an intensity. The substituted answer has the same intensity, projected onto whatever scale was asked (dollars, years, ratings).

**2. The mood heuristic for life satisfaction**
> "How happy are you these days?" → "What is my mood right now?" (lines 3671–3725)

When the dating question was asked BEFORE the happiness question, correlation between dating and happiness was 0.66. When asked AFTER, it was 0.0. The dating question primed an emotional state that got substituted for the happiness judgment.

**3. The affect heuristic**
> "Your emotional attitude... drives your beliefs about benefits and risks." (lines 3730–3739)

When judging the risks/benefits of nuclear power, food irradiation, etc., people don't compute. They feel their like/dislike and substitute that for the analytical answer.

## How to apply

When you have a quick, confident answer to a hard question:

1. **Ask: what's the TARGET question?** Write it out.
2. **Ask: what EASIER question might System 1 have substituted?** Often it's an emotional intensity question.
3. **Check the answers against each other.** If your "judgment" of long-term consequences happens to match your current mood, you probably substituted.
4. **For high-stakes judgments: deliberately answer the hard question.** Decompose. Compute. Apply a checklist. See references/kahneman-taming-intuitive-predictions.md.
5. **In survey/poll design: be aware that respondents will substitute.** "How satisfied are you with life?" reliably picks up current mood, not life evaluation.

## Common failure modes

- **Treating the felt-as-answer as the actual answer.** "I FELT certain it would work" is data about your emotion, not about the outcome.
- **Substitution + WYSIATI bundled.** You substitute an easier question, answer it from limited info, and feel confident. Triple failure with one move.
- **Believing introspection will catch it.** "I checked my answer; it feels right" — that's System 1 endorsing System 1. Need external structure to catch substitution.
