# Galef: Lean In To Confusion

## When to Use

Use when reality is not fitting the map:

- A metric contradicts the story.
- A customer, market, or seller behavior seems irrational.
- A stock thesis has a data point that does not fit.
- The team wants to dismiss an anomaly as noise.

## The Framework

Galef argues confusion is a clue. Scouts do not rush to make anomalies disappear. Darwin's "golden rule" was to record facts that contradicted his theories because those were easiest to forget.

## How to Apply

1. **Record the anomaly.** Write it before explaining it away.
2. **Name the current story it threatens.**
3. **List possible explanations.** Include boring, adverse, and surprising ones.
4. **Score spring tension.** How much does the current story have to stretch?
5. **Decide whether to stay confused.** If evidence is insufficient, preserve uncertainty.
6. **Choose next observation.** What would clarify the anomaly?

## Output Format

```markdown
## Lean In To Confusion: {anomaly}

Anomaly:
Threatened story:
Possible explanations:
Spring-stretch score:
What not to conclude yet:
Next observation:
```

## Common Failure Modes

- **Premature explanation.** The first plausible story becomes the answer.
- **Noise dismissal.** "Probably nothing" becomes a reflex.
- **Overfit anomaly.** One odd point rewrites the whole model.
- **No anomaly log.** Confusing evidence disappears from memory.
