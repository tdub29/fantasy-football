## Em Dashes: The Primary AI Tell

**The em dash (—) has become one of the most reliable markers of AI-generated content.**

Em dashes are longer than hyphens (-) and are used for emphasis, interruptions, or parenthetical information. While they have legitimate uses in writing, AI models drastically overuse them.

### Why Em Dashes Signal AI Writing
- AI models were trained on edited books, academic papers, and style guides where em dashes appear frequently
- AI uses em dashes as a shortcut for sentence variety instead of commas, colons, or parentheses
- Most human writers rarely use em dashes because they don't exist as a standard keyboard key
- The overuse is so consistent that it has become the unofficial signature of ChatGPT writing

### What To Do Instead
| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| The results—which were surprising—showed... | The results, which were surprising, showed... |
| This approach—unlike traditional methods—allows... | This approach, unlike traditional methods, allows... |
| The study found—as expected—that... | The study found, as expected, that... |
| Communication skills—both written and verbal—are essential | Communication skills (both written and verbal) are essential |

### Guidelines
- Use commas for most parenthetical information
- Use colons to introduce explanations or lists
- Use parentheses for supplementary information
- Reserve em dashes for rare, deliberate emphasis only
- If you find yourself using more than one em dash per page, revise

## Phrases That Signal AI Writing

### Opening Phrases to Avoid
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In today's digital age..."
- "In an era of..."
- "In the ever-evolving landscape of..."
- "In the realm of..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "Let's delve into..."
- "Imagine a world where..."

### Transitional Phrases to Avoid
- "That being said..."
- "With that in mind..."
- "It's worth mentioning that..."
- "At its core..."
- "To put it simply..."
- "In essence..."
- "This begs the question..."

### Concluding Phrases to Avoid
- "In conclusion..."
- "To sum up..."
- "By [doing X], you can [achieve Y]..."
- "In the final analysis..."
- "All things considered..."
- "At the end of the day..."

### Structural Patterns to Avoid
- "Whether you're a [X], [Y], or [Z]..." (listing three examples after "whether")
- "It's not just [X], it's also [Y]..."
- "Think of [X] as [elaborate metaphor]..."
- Starting sentences with "By" followed by a gerund: "By understanding X, you can Y..."

## Trevor / Job-Search Application Copy — Flagged Tells (always cut)

Patterns Trevor has repeatedly caught in his resumes, cover letters, LinkedIn, and cold emails. Treat these as hard fails in any external, candidate-facing copy and cut them in every draft and self-critique pass, without waiting to be told. (These are *in addition* to the no-em-dashes, no-hedges, and filler-vs-differentiator rules.)

| Tell | Why it's weak | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Fourth-wall / JD-narrating** — "hits all three of the deliverables this role names," "exactly what you're looking for," "I'm the perfect fit," "as this role requires" | Talks *about* the posting / grades your own match; reads as obviously tailored and lets the reader off the hook | State the work; let them conclude the fit. (A light research signal in a *cover letter* — "I read your RedditEng post," "your posting's line about X stood out" — is fine; never self-grade against the requirements, and never narrate the JD in a *resume*.) |
| **Protesting honesty** — "and I would not pretend otherwise," "to be honest," "I won't sugarcoat it," "full transparency" | Editorializing about your own honesty reads as defensive | Make the concession plainly and pivot to the strength. Concession-then-pivot is good; the meta-honesty clause is the tell. |
| **Throat-clearing bridges & the "not just" scaffold** — "less about demos, more about workflow fit," "it's not just about X, it's about Y," **the bare contrast in any form: "X, not just Y" / "not just the reports on top of them" / "not only … but …" / "not merely"**, "at its heart my work is about..." | Abstract setup that delays the concrete; the "not just / not only" contrast is a grating AI tic (Trevor 2026-06-24: "not just drives me nuts") | Cut it. Lead with the concrete artifact + number, or **state the positive directly** without the contrast scaffold ("I catch data problems in the pipeline" beats "I test pipelines, not just the reports"). Test: if anyone in the role could write the sentence, cut it. |
| **Hedge openers / softeners** — "From what I can tell," "seems to," "I would guess," "if I'm honest," "I think," "kind of," "really" (on a claim) | Undermines confidence in your own work | Confident and direct. Honest *gaps* stated plainly are fine; hedged *confidence* is not. |
| **"real" as an intensifier** — "real operating workflows," "real customer-data workflow," "real impact" | Empty intensifier / AI tell | Use the concrete noun, or "production" / "live" when literally true. |
| **Waffling on settled numbers** — re-hedging a figure's precision in the copy | Reads as unsure; annoys Trevor | Once a figure is approved (e.g. "20 departments / 1,000+ average monthly views"), use it. Don't re-derive or hedge it. |
| **Undefendable jargon / insider terms** — "across runtimes," "model-invoked" (unglossed); **internal Good360 product/channel names a recruiter won't know (e.g., "Retail Match," "Pallet Equivalents (PE)," "TLE")**; overloaded internal shorthand | The reader (or Trevor in an interview) can't crisply define it; internal proper nouns mean nothing to an external audience | Cut it, or make it concrete. For internal Good360 names, use a plain descriptor ("a retail-donation channel," "standardized volume units"). The test: would he have to pause to explain it, or would a recruiter not recognize it? Then rewrite it. |
| **Agentless passive / ownership-by-assertion on your own work** — "a ~$130M income statement needed reclassifying," "the model was drifting," "that was mine to fix," "the work was owned end to end," "decisions were made to..." | Dodges the verb *you* did; describes work happening *to* you instead of *by* you; "that was mine to fix" asserts ownership instead of showing the action. Reads as evasive AI prose. | Make Trevor the grammatical subject doing the action verb: "I cleaned and reclassified roughly two years of a ~$130M departmental income statement at the source." Test: any sentence about his accomplishment where he is **not** the subject doing the verb = rewrite it active. |

After the read-aloud pass, scan specifically for these seven. Any hit = revise before calling the draft ready. **For the passive/agentless one:** check every sentence that describes Trevor's work and confirm he is the subject doing the verb; if the work is the subject ("X needed doing") or ownership is merely asserted ("that was mine"), rewrite it active. **Also literally grep for "not just" and "not only"** — the bare contrast scaffold is an AI tic Trevor flags; rewrite to state the positive directly.

## How to Self-Check

1. Read your text aloud. If phrases sound unnatural in speech, revise them
2. Ask: "Would I say this in a conversation with a colleague?"
3. Check for repetitive sentence structures
4. Look for clusters of the words listed above
5. Ensure varied sentence lengths (not all similar length)
6. Verify each intensifier adds genuine meaning
